Café Notes

A good table, a slow morning, and a phone that just works

Notes on where to sit with a coffee or something sweet, in 30 places. Below them, short guides to mobile data for each destination.

* * *

  • Barcelona, SpainThe best bakeries and sweet stops in Barcelona

    Barcelona has old Catalan pastry traditions and a young specialty-coffee scene, often on the same street. These notes are about finding both without spending the day crossing the city.

  • Beijing, ChinaThe best bakeries and sweet stops in Beijing

    Beijing's café scene is a study in contrast: coffee in an old courtyard house down a narrow hutong, or in a glossy shopping district a few metro stops away. Both are worth an afternoon.

  • Bugis, SingaporeThe best bakeries and sweet stops in Bugis

    Bugis is compact but busy, and Singapore's heat shapes how you use it. Air-conditioned malls make an easy base, while the quieter streets nearby are better for a long, slow coffee.

  • Innsbruck, AustriaThe best bakeries and sweet stops in Innsbruck

    Austria takes its coffee-and-cake hour seriously, and Innsbruck, with mountains at the end of every street, is a lovely place to observe the ritual.

  • Yogyakarta, IndonesiaThe best bakeries and sweet stops in Yogyakarta

    Yogyakarta has a big café culture, from traditional coffee stalls to spacious modern cafés. Its local sweets, especially bakpia, deserve a stop of their own.

  • York, United KingdomThe best bakeries and sweet stops in York

    York does tea rooms well. Its medieval streets are full of places for a pot of tea and a slice of cake, and a few independent coffee shops for when you want something stronger.

  • Bohol, PhilippinesThe best cafés in Bohol for travelers

    Bohol's cafés are scattered across an island, not packed into a city. The best approach is to enjoy the ones near where you are staying and treat the rest as part of a day out.

  • Busan, South KoreaThe best cafés in Busan for travelers

    Busan's café culture runs from dense backstreets to cliff-top spaces with ocean views. Choose one area per afternoon and the city feels easy.

  • Chinatown, SingaporeThe best cafés in Singapore's Chinatown for travelers

    Chinatown is where Singapore's old coffee culture meets the new. In a single morning you can drink kopi from a traditional coffee shop and a flat white in a restored shophouse.

  • Dresden, GermanyThe best cafés in Dresden for travelers

    Dresden has two café personalities, divided by the Elbe. The Altstadt keeps the tradition of coffee and cake; the Neustadt across the river has the independent coffee scene.

  • Kuala Lumpur, MalaysiaThe best cafés in Kuala Lumpur for travelers

    Kuala Lumpur's café scene is spread across neighbourhoods divided by traffic and rail lines. Pick one area per outing and use the train or a ride between them.

  • Shinjuku, JapanThe best cafés in Shinjuku for travelers

    Shinjuku is one of the busiest places in Tokyo, which makes a good café all the more welcome. The trick is choosing the right side of the station before you start.

  • Abu Dhabi, United Arab EmiratesThe best coffee shops in Abu Dhabi

    Abu Dhabi is spread out and hot, so coffee here is about choosing a district and settling in, not hopping between cafés on foot.

  • Amsterdam, NetherlandsThe best coffee shops in Amsterdam, the ones that serve coffee

    A word of warning first. In Amsterdam, a "coffeeshop" usually means a licensed cannabis venue. If you are after espresso and a pastry, you want a café or a koffiebar.

  • Bordeaux, FranceThe best coffee shops in Bordeaux

    Bordeaux is a wine city, but its coffee scene has grown, and its pastry is excellent. Mornings here are for a coffee on a terrace and a canelé still warm from the bakery.

  • Hoi An, VietnamThe best coffee shops in Hoi An

    Vietnamese coffee is strong, sweet and slow to drip, which suits Hoi An perfectly. The best cafés here are usually a street or two away from the busiest lanes.

  • Pattaya, ThailandThe best coffee shops in Pattaya

    Pattaya is better known for its nightlife, but its café scene has grown up, with garden cafés, hillside terraces and serious coffee along the coast.

  • Venice, ItalyThe best coffee shops in Venice

    In Venice, as in the rest of Italy, coffee is usually drunk standing at the bar, quickly, for a fraction of the price of a table on a famous square. Knowing that changes how you use the city's cafés.

  • Chengdu, ChinaA China café guide for slow travel days, from Chengdu

    Chengdu is famous for its relaxed pace and its tea houses, where locals spend whole afternoons. Its modern cafés follow the same spirit, which makes it the right city for a slow day.

  • Bologna, ItalyAn Italy café guide for slow travel days, from Bologna

    Italian café life happens at the bar: a quick espresso, a pastry, a few words with the barista, and back out into the day. Bologna, with its covered porticoes, is a lovely place to do it slowly.

  • Valencia, SpainA Spain café guide for slow travel days, from Valencia

    Spanish café life runs through the whole day: coffee in the morning, a drink in the afternoon, something sweet in the evening. In Valencia, the afternoon belongs to horchata.

  • Lucerne, SwitzerlandA Switzerland café guide for slow travel days, from Lucerne

    Swiss cafés are comfortable, punctual and not cheap. In Lucerne, the main choice is simple: pay for the lake view, or walk a street back and spend less.

  • Antalya, TurkeyA Turkey café guide for slow travel days, from Antalya

    Turkish café culture runs on tea as much as coffee. In Antalya, you can sit with a tulip glass of çay in a stone courtyard in the old town, then walk down to the sea for something sweet.

  • Ho Chi Minh City, VietnamA Vietnam café guide for slow travel days, from Ho Chi Minh City

    Ho Chi Minh City has one of the world's great café cultures. Some of its best cafés hide upstairs in old apartment blocks or at the end of narrow alleys, which is part of the fun.

  • Gangnam, South KoreaWhere to find dessert in Gangnam

    Gangnam's dessert cafés are polished, inventive and spread across several neighbourhoods. Choose one cluster per afternoon; trying to cover them all in one go means more time on the subway than at the table.

  • Lyon, FranceWhere to find dessert in Lyon

    Lyon's food reputation extends to its pastry. Its signature colour is the bright pink of praline, and its pâtisseries and chocolatiers are among the best in France.

  • Melaka, MalaysiaWhere to find dessert in Melaka

    Melaka's desserts are built on two local treasures: gula Melaka, dark palm sugar, and coconut. They come together in the city's famous cendol, best eaten on a hot afternoon in the old town.

  • Miami, United StatesWhere to find dessert in Miami

    Miami's sweet side is Cuban bakeries, tropical flavours and the tart key lime pie of South Florida. Start in Little Havana in the morning and the rest of the day follows.

  • Phuket, ThailandWhere to find dessert in Phuket

    Phuket's sweet side mixes Thai desserts with the Peranakan traditions of its Old Town, plus beach cafés with sunset views. It is an island, not a city, so plan by area.

  • Sydney, AustraliaWhere to find dessert in Sydney

    Sydney's dessert scene follows its coffee culture: early starts, excellent bakeries and Asian dessert shops in Haymarket. Go early; the best pastries sell out.
