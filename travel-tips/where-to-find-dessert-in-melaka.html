Melaka

Where to find dessert in Melaka

Melaka's desserts are built on two local treasures: gula Melaka, dark palm sugar, and coconut. They come together in the city's famous cendol, best eaten on a hot afternoon in the old town.

Published July 19, 2025 · Updated July 26, 2025 · 3 minutes

* * *

Jonker Street and the heritage lanes

The heart of the old town has cendol shops, Nyonya kuih makers and modern cafés in restored shophouses. Busy at weekends, especially in the evening.

The riverside

The Melaka River walk has cafés for a cool drink or a dessert while you watch the boats.

Klebang

West of the centre, Klebang is known for its coconut shakes, a short ride from the old town.

What to order

DessertWhat it is
CendolShaved ice with coconut milk, gula Melaka syrup and green rice-flour jelly
Durian cendolCendol with durian, for those who love it
Nyonya kuihSmall Peranakan cakes, often coloured with pandan and butterfly pea flower
Coconut shakeCoconut milk and flesh blended with ice cream

Only order durian cendol if you know you like durian. Its flavour and smell are strong.

When to go

Weekday mornings are easiest. At weekends, get your café stops in before the evening crowd builds around Jonker Street.

What it costs

Before publication, modern café coffee was roughly RM8 to RM16, and many local desserts RM5 to RM15. Simple cendol can be cheaper.

Practical notes

Jonker is compact but crowded at weekends. Walk between the heritage streets, and use ride-hailing only for outer areas such as Klebang. Not every cendol shop has comfortable seating; some are for a quick bowl and back on your way.

A few questions

What is gula Melaka?

A dark palm sugar, used in cendol and many Malaysian desserts.

Where can I get a coconut shake in Melaka?

Klebang, west of the old town, is known for them.

When is Jonker Street least crowded?

On weekday mornings.
