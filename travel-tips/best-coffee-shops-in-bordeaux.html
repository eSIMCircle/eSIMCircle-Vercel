The best coffee shops in Bordeaux

Bordeaux is a wine city, but its coffee scene has grown, and its pastry is excellent. Mornings here are for a coffee on a terrace and a canelé still warm from the bakery.

Published February 23, 2026 · Updated August 15, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Chartrons

North along the river, Chartrons is relaxed, with coffee roasters, terraces and bakeries around Rue Notre-Dame. The best area for specialty coffee.

Saint-Pierre

The historic streets near the Place du Parlement have plenty of coffee and pastry choices, close to the sights.

Saint-Michel

Around the basilica, Saint-Michel is diverse and less polished, with casual coffee and everyday bakeries.

The Triangle d'Or

Between the Grand Théâtre and the main avenues, the "golden triangle" is the place for refined pâtisserie and classic French café breaks.

What to order

The canelé is Bordeaux's signature: a small cake with a dark caramelised crust and a soft, custardy centre. Eat it fresh, the same day. A chocolatine, as the pain au chocolat is called in much of south-west France, is the obvious breakfast partner.

Canelés are best early in the day, when bakeries have the full selection. Do not leave them until the evening.

When to go

Coffee shops are calmest in the morning. Bakeries have the best selection before lunch, and terraces fill on sunny afternoons.

What it costs

Before publication, coffee was roughly €2.50 to €5 and pastry or cake €3 to €7. Refined pâtisserie costs more.

A short route

Bordeaux's centre is walkable, and the tram helps with longer hops. Start in Chartrons with coffee, walk along the river to Saint-Pierre for a canelé, and finish with a terrace break. If you are also visiting wine country, go easy on the pastries; it is a lot of richness for one day.
