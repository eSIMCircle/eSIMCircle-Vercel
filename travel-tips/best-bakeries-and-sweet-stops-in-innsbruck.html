The best bakeries and sweet stops in Innsbruck

Austria takes its coffee-and-cake hour seriously, and Innsbruck, with mountains at the end of every street, is a lovely place to observe the ritual.

Published September 21, 2026 · Updated October 9, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

The Old Town

The compact Altstadt, around the Golden Roof, has traditional cafés serving coffee with strudel and cake in wood-panelled rooms. It is the obvious first stop and busiest in the afternoon.

Maria-Theresien-Straße

The main street running south from the Old Town is lined with cafés and bakeries. It is easy to combine a coffee with a walk through the centre, with the Nordkette mountains rising behind you.

Wilten

South of the centre, Wilten is calmer, with modern coffee and a neighbourhood feel. Take the tram or walk if you want a break from the visitor crowds.

Saggen

East of the centre, Saggen's leafy streets have less tourist traffic and suit a slow afternoon coffee.

What to order

SweetWhat it is
ApfelstrudelThin pastry rolled around apples, cinnamon and raisins, often served warm with cream
KaiserschmarrnA shredded, caramelised pancake with fruit compote, almost a meal
SachertorteThe famous Viennese chocolate cake, common across Austria
TopfenstrudelStrudel with a sweet quark filling

Order a Melange, the Austrian cousin of the cappuccino, to go with it.

Traditional cafés are made for lingering over coffee and cake, but some prefer shorter stays at busy times. Keep the laptop for a modern café.

When to go

Morning bakeries are calmer. Traditional cake cafés fill up in the afternoon, especially on wet days, when everyone has the same idea.

What it costs

Before publication, a coffee at a sit-down café was roughly €3 to €5 and a slice of cake or pastry €4 to €8.

A gentle afternoon

Walk the Old Town in the morning, stop for strudel around three, then take the tram to Wilten or walk to Saggen for a quieter second coffee. Do not order dense pastries at every stop; one is plenty.

A few questions

What should I order in an Innsbruck café?

Apfelstrudel or Kaiserschmarrn with a Melange, the Austrian version of a cappuccino.

When are Innsbruck's cafés busiest?

In the afternoon, especially on rainy days.

What is Kaiserschmarrn?

A shredded, caramelised pancake served with fruit compote.
