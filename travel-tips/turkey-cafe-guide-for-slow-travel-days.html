Antalya

A Turkey café guide for slow travel days, from Antalya

Turkish café culture runs on tea as much as coffee. In Antalya, you can sit with a tulip glass of çay in a stone courtyard in the old town, then walk down to the sea for something sweet.

Published September 21, 2026 · Updated October 9, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Kaleiçi

The old town's lanes are full of small cafés, Turkish coffee and sweet shops. Courtyard cafés are lovely, though seating can be tight. Go early, before the heat and crowds.

Konyaaltı

West along the beach, modern cafés and dessert shops work well before or after time by the sea.

Lara

On the eastern side of the city, Lara has larger, newer cafés and bakeries.

Muratpaşa

In the central district beyond the old town, simpler coffee and pastry stops serve locals at lower prices.

What to order

OrderWhat it is
ÇayBlack tea in a small tulip-shaped glass, Turkey's everyday drink
Türk kahvesiTurkish coffee, thick and unfiltered, served in a small cup
BaklavaLayers of filo with nuts and syrup
KünefeShredded pastry with cheese, soaked in syrup and served warm

Let Turkish coffee settle before drinking, and stop before you reach the grounds at the bottom.

When to go

Kaleiçi is pleasant early. Seaside cafés are best later in the day, and dessert shops stay lively into the evening.

What it costs

Prices in Turkey change quickly, so old online figures are unreliable. Compare menu boards when you arrive. Cafés in Kaleiçi, Lara and on the beachfront are usually noticeably more expensive than neighbourhood bakeries.

Getting around

Antalya is bigger than its old town. Use the tram, a bus or ride-hailing to reach Konyaaltı and Lara rather than walking long, exposed stretches at midday. They are on opposite sides of the city, so do not plan both into a short afternoon. And not every baklava shop has seating; some are for takeaway only.

A few questions

How do I drink Turkish coffee?

Let it settle, sip slowly and stop before the grounds at the bottom of the cup.

What is künefe?

Shredded pastry with cheese, soaked in syrup and served warm.

Are Lara and Konyaaltı close together?

No. They are on opposite sides of Antalya.
