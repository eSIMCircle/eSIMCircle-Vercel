The best cafés in Shinjuku for travelers

Shinjuku is one of the busiest places in Tokyo, which makes a good café all the more welcome. The trick is choosing the right side of the station before you start.

Published September 16, 2025 · Updated September 17, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Shinjuku Sanchome and the east side

Department stores, side streets and café buildings make this the densest area for coffee and desserts. Many cafés are on upper floors.

The South Exit and New South Gate

Modern complexes and wide walkways make the south side simpler for first-time visitors.

Nishi-Shinjuku

The west side, among the skyscrapers, has hotel cafés, specialty coffee and more spacious seating away from the east-side crowds.

Kagurazaka

A short train ride away, Kagurazaka has stone lanes and traditional Japanese sweet shops. Worth a separate trip if you want a calmer neighbourhood.

What to order

Japanese cafés are known for careful pour-over coffee, thick fluffy pancakes and elaborate parfaits. For traditional sweets, try wagashi with matcha in a Japanese tea house, or anmitsu, a dessert of agar jelly, fruit and sweet bean paste.

Department-store dessert floors are worth a look even if you do not sit down. Their displays are a lesson in Japanese sweets.

When to go

Before 11am is easiest. Department-store cafés and dessert floors get busy after lunch and at weekends.

What it costs

Before publication, coffee was roughly ¥450 to ¥900 and a plated dessert or parfait ¥700 to ¥1,600 in central Shinjuku.

Exits matter

Shinjuku Station is enormous. Save the exact exit and building name for each café; one that looks 300 metres away can take far longer if you come up on the wrong side. Check whether a café is at street level or on an upper floor, and check the menu before joining a long dessert queue.

A few questions

Which side of Shinjuku Station has the most cafés?

The east side, around Shinjuku Sanchome, has the densest choice.

Where can I find traditional Japanese sweets near Shinjuku?

Kagurazaka, a short train ride away, has traditional sweet shops and tea houses.

Are Shinjuku cafés at street level?

Many are on upper floors of buildings. Check the floor before you go.
