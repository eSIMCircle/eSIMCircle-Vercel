Chinatown

The best cafés in Singapore's Chinatown for travelers

Chinatown is where Singapore's old coffee culture meets the new. In a single morning you can drink kopi from a traditional coffee shop and a flat white in a restored shophouse.

Published July 17, 2025 · Updated December 20, 2025 · 3 minutes

* * *

Kreta Ayer and Smith Street

In the historic core, traditional bakeries and coffee shops sit next to modern cafés. This is the place to start with local coffee.

Keong Saik

A short walk west, Keong Saik Road's restored shophouses hold coffee shops, bakeries and modern dessert places.

Duxton and the Tanjong Pagar edge

Towards Tanjong Pagar, Duxton has more contemporary coffee options in a quieter setting.

Ann Siang and the Telok Ayer edge

The small hill of Ann Siang and nearby Telok Ayer have pastries and coffee. Best once the office lunch rush has passed.

Ordering local coffee

Traditional Singapore coffee, kopi, is made with robusta beans roasted with sugar and margarine and served with condensed or evaporated milk. The ordering vocabulary is part of the fun:

OrderWhat you get
KopiCoffee with condensed milk
Kopi-OBlack coffee with sugar
Kopi-CCoffee with evaporated milk and sugar
Kopi-O kosongBlack coffee, no sugar

Pair it with kaya toast and soft-boiled eggs for a classic breakfast.

Kopi is strong and sweet. If you prefer less sugar, add "siu dai" (less sweet) to your order.

When to go

Morning is ideal for traditional coffee and bakeries. Cafés around Telok Ayer and Tanjong Pagar get busy with office workers at lunch.

What it costs

Before publication, a café coffee was about S$4 to S$8 and most café desserts S$5 to S$14. Traditional kopi is much cheaper.

A short morning route

Start in Kreta Ayer with kopi and kaya toast, walk to Keong Saik for a bakery or modern café, then finish at the Tanjong Pagar edge if you want specialty coffee. Save street numbers and floor levels; some shophouse cafés are upstairs.

A few questions

What is kopi?

Traditional Singapore coffee, made with robusta beans and usually served with condensed milk.

What does kopi-O mean?

Black coffee with sugar. Add "kosong" for no sugar.

What is kaya toast?

Toast with kaya, a coconut and egg jam, often eaten with soft-boiled eggs and kopi.
