The best bakeries and sweet stops in Bugis

Bugis is compact but busy, and Singapore's heat shapes how you use it. Air-conditioned malls make an easy base, while the quieter streets nearby are better for a long, slow coffee.

Published January 23, 2026 · Updated September 6, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Bugis Junction and Bugis+

The malls next to the MRT station make an easy base for coffee, cakes, bingsu and bakery stops, all in the cool. Useful at midday, when walking outside is hard work.

Middle Road and Bencoolen

A short walk from Bugis, these streets have quieter cafés, good for a longer break away from the crowds.

The Haji Lane edge

On the edge of Kampong Glam, Haji Lane has compact, design-led cafés. They are charming but small, and seating fills quickly after lunch.

Bras Basah

Towards the museums, Bras Basah suits a calmer coffee away from the busiest shopping streets.

What to order

Singapore's café menus mix local and international flavours. Look for kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs and local coffee for a traditional start. Modern cafés serve pandan and gula melaka cakes, matcha desserts and Korean-style bingsu, shaved ice piled with toppings.

In the heat, go easy on rich desserts. One cold sweet and a drink is usually enough for a stop.

When to go

Weekday mornings and early afternoons are easiest. Mall cafés get busy after work and at weekends, and the small Haji Lane spaces fill up after lunch.

What it costs

Before publication, a typical coffee was about S$4 to S$8 and a dessert S$6 to S$15, with premium matcha or plated sweets at the upper end.

Finding your way

Bugis is compact but multi-level. A listing might be on the fourth floor of a mall, not the street. Check the exact level or unit, and note the nearest MRT exit before you head out into the heat. Use covered or underground routes where you can.

A few questions

Where can I get a coffee in Bugis out of the heat?

The Bugis Junction and Bugis+ malls next to the MRT station have many air-conditioned cafés.

What is bingsu?

A Korean shaved-ice dessert piled with toppings, popular in Singapore cafés.

When is Haji Lane least crowded?

In the morning, before the small cafés fill up after lunch.
