Where to find dessert in Miami

Miami's sweet side is Cuban bakeries, tropical flavours and the tart key lime pie of South Florida. Start in Little Havana in the morning and the rest of the day follows.

Published February 3, 2026 · Updated February 3, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Little Havana

Cuban bakeries and ventanitas along Calle Ocho are the place for pastelitos and a cafecito. Best in the morning.

Wynwood

The arts district has dessert shops, ice cream and bakeries among the murals. Quieter earlier in the day.

South Beach

Beachfront cafés and dessert spots, busier from late morning onwards and often pricier, especially with table service.

Coconut Grove

A leafy neighbourhood with relaxed cafés and bakeries away from the busiest areas.

What to order

SweetWhat it is
Pastelito de guayabaA flaky pastry filled with guava, often with cream cheese
CafecitoStrong, sweet Cuban espresso, often shared in tiny cups
Key lime pieA tart South Florida classic with a graham-cracker crust
FlanCaramel custard, common in Cuban and Latin American bakeries
Tres lechesA sponge cake soaked in three kinds of milk

A cafecito is much stronger than a drip coffee. A colada is a larger serving meant for sharing in small cups.

When to go

Little Havana in the morning, Wynwood before it gets busy, South Beach earlier rather than later.

What it costs

Before publication, coffee was around US$4 to US$7 and desserts US$6 to US$12. Sit-down South Beach venues cost more before tax and tip. The final bill in the US usually adds sales tax, and table service usually expects a tip.

Getting around

Miami's neighbourhoods are spread out. Use rideshare or transit between them; do not try to walk from Little Havana to South Beach.

A few questions

What is a pastelito?

A flaky Cuban pastry, often filled with guava, cheese or meat.

What is a colada?

A larger serving of Cuban espresso, meant to be shared in small cups.

Are prices in Miami cafés final?

Usually not. Sales tax is added, and table service typically expects a tip.
