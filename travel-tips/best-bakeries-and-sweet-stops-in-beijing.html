The best bakeries and sweet stops in Beijing

Beijing's café scene is a study in contrast: coffee in an old courtyard house down a narrow hutong, or in a glossy shopping district a few metro stops away. Both are worth an afternoon.

Published January 23, 2026 · Updated August 3, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Gulou and the hutong lanes

Around the Drum and Bell Towers, the old hutong neighbourhoods hide small cafés in courtyard houses, some with just a few tables. These are the places for a slow break, especially in late morning when the lanes are quiet.

Hutong cafés are often hard to see from the street. A map pin may land on the main road while the entrance is down a lane. Save the Chinese name and address of each place, and look for small signs on doorways.

Wudaoying and the Lama Temple area

A short walk from the Lama Temple, Wudaoying Hutong is a compact stretch of independent coffee, tea and dessert spots. It pairs naturally with a temple visit.

Sanlitun

East of the centre, Sanlitun is Beijing's international district. Large brands, local roasters and polished dessert cafés are easy to combine, and it stays busy into the evening.

Guomao and the CBD

In the business district, mall cafés and bakeries are practical in extreme weather, whether it is a hot summer afternoon or a cold winter day.

What to order

Alongside espresso drinks, look for Chinese-influenced flavours in modern cafés: osmanthus, red bean, black sesame and tea-based drinks. Traditional Beijing pastries, such as sesame cakes and sweets made with hawthorn, are sold in long-established pastry shops.

When to go

Hutong cafés are at their nicest in late morning. Sanlitun gets busier after lunch and at weekends.

What it costs

Before publication, a modern coffee was roughly ¥25 to ¥45 and a café dessert ¥30 to ¥70, with premium hotel or concept venues higher.

Do not try to walk from Sanlitun to the central hutongs as part of one café crawl. They are far apart; use the metro.

Practical notes

Many cafés take Alipay or WeChat Pay, and some use mobile ordering. Small hutong cafés may have very limited seating, so avoid arriving with a large group. Translation apps help with menus in Chinese only.

A few questions

Where are the best hutong cafés in Beijing?

Around Gulou, near the Drum and Bell Towers, and along Wudaoying Hutong near the Lama Temple.

Why is it hard to find hutong cafés?

Entrances are often down narrow lanes with small signs. Save the Chinese name and address before you go.

How do I pay in Beijing cafés?

Many use Alipay or WeChat Pay. Set one up before your trip.
