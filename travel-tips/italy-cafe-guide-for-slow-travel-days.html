An Italy café guide for slow travel days, from Bologna

Italian café life happens at the bar: a quick espresso, a pastry, a few words with the barista, and back out into the day. Bologna, with its covered porticoes, is a lovely place to do it slowly.

Published June 28, 2026 · Updated August 25, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

How Italian bars work

In Italy, a "bar" is often a café. Most people drink standing at the counter, which is usually the cheapest way. Sitting at a table, especially in a central square, can cost more because of table service. Check before you sit.

The centre and the Quadrilatero edge

Bars and pastry counters are dense and convenient around Piazza Maggiore and the old market streets. The most central tables can be busy and pricier.

The university quarter

Lower-key cafés and bakeries serve students and residents, with a more everyday rhythm.

Via Saragozza

A westward route under long porticoes, with independent bakeries and quieter places to sit.

Pratello

Independent cafés make the area around Via del Pratello a comfortable place to slow down.

What to order

TimeOrder
MorningCappuccino and a cornetto (Italian croissant)
Late morningAn espresso at the bar
AfternoonAn espresso or macchiato, rarely a cappuccino

Bologna also has seasonal sweets, such as certosino, a spiced Christmas cake, so look out for what is in the bakery window.

Many Italians drink cappuccino only before late morning. Ordering one after lunch is fine, just unusual.

When to go

Early morning is the heart of bar culture. Pastry counters stay busy until late morning, and sit-down cafés fill after lunch.

What it costs

Before publication, a standing espresso was around €1.50 to €3 and pastries often €2 to €5. Table service and central tourist spots cost more.

A slow route

Use the porticoes. They keep you dry in the rain and shaded in summer, so you can wander between a few cafés without crossing the whole city for one bakery.

A few questions

Is it cheaper to stand at the bar in Italy?

Usually, yes. Table service, especially in central squares, often costs more.

When do Italians drink cappuccino?

Mostly in the morning.

What is a cornetto?

The Italian croissant, often eaten with a cappuccino at breakfast.
