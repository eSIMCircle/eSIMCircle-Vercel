A first trip to Moldova, with the phone ready

A first trip to Moldova is easier with one less thing to figure out on arrival.

Published August 16, 2026 · Updated September 10, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Usage patterns that change the best choice

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For 7-day, 15-day, and 30-day plan timing, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

Mistakes to avoid before checkout

The risky shortcut is buying the cheapest plan before checking the boring details. Confirm that the phone is eSIM compatible and carrier-unlocked, then check whether the plan covers the full route, the full date range, and the kind of use implied by 7-day, 15-day, and 30-day plan timing. A little extra checking before checkout is easier than troubleshooting with no data after arrival.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

The Moldova plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Moldova has 9 plans: 3 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 1.89 to 6.09 USD, with allowances from 1GB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.99 to 17.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Moldova eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 0.90 USD per GB and 0.60 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (1GB per day) comes to 18.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 60.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

When should I install it?

At home, on good Wi-Fi, a day or so before leaving.

What is the least a Moldova plan costs here?

1.89 USD, for Moldova eSIM 1GB/Day.

How does the eSIM arrive?

By email, with a QR code and instructions. Keep the email until you are home.

How many plans are there to choose from?

9. 3 reset daily and 6 hold a fixed amount.

Can the home SIM stay in the phone?

Yes. Keep it for calls and messages and use the eSIM for data.

Can I buy for the whole group at once?

Yes. Several eSIMs can be bought in one purchase.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.
