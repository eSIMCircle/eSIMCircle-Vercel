Line selection, roaming, and hotspot settings

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For common coverage and activation questions answered, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

What can go wrong if the plan is chosen too quickly

The risky shortcut is buying the cheapest plan before checking the boring details. Confirm that the phone is eSIM compatible and carrier-unlocked, then check whether the plan covers the full route, the full date range, and the kind of use implied by common coverage and activation questions answered. A little extra checking before checkout is easier than troubleshooting with no data after arrival.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

  • Install on Wi-Fi before the trip when possible.
  • Keep the QR email until the trip is over.
  • Set the eSIM as the mobile data line, not necessarily the voice line.
  • Check that the device is carrier-unlocked and eSIM compatible.

The Asia (20 areas) plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Asia (20 areas) has 9 plans: 3 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 3.29 to 8.49 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 2GB per day. Fixed plans run from 8.55 to 71.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Asia eSIM (20 Areas) 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 3.60 USD per GB and 2.40 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 32.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (2GB per day) to 84.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

Who are daily plans for?

Travelers whose phones work hard every day: a laptop on hotspot, video calls, regular uploads.

What is the least an Asia (20 areas) plan costs here?

3.29 USD, for Asia eSIM (20 Areas) 500MB/Day.

May I share the connection?

Each plan has its own hotspot rules, listed on the plan page.

How many plans are there to choose from?

9. 3 reset daily and 6 hold a fixed amount.

Can the home SIM stay in the phone?

Yes. Keep it for calls and messages and use the eSIM for data.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.

Does the phone need anything special?

It needs eSIM support and must be unlocked by your home carrier.
