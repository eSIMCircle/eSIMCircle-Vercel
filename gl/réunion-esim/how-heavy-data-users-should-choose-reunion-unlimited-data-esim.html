A Réunion eSIM for days that use a lot of data

Some travel days in Réunion are light. Some involve a laptop, a hotspot and a video call. Plan for the second kind.

Published August 11, 2025 · Updated September 4, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

A simple framework for choosing the right option

This section keeps the focus on comparing data plans before checkout for Réunion. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

Examples that make the decision clearer

Read the plan cards as a fit check, not a scoreboard. Fixed-data plans are useful when your schedule and data habits are predictable. Daily or flexible plans are better when some days may be heavier than others. Compare Réunion eSIM options by validity, data ceiling, hotspot expectations, and setup timing before price becomes the deciding factor.

Plans are sold in steps of days, and trips rarely match them exactly. When a trip falls between two steps, the longer plan is usually the safer choice, because a plan can be extended while it is active but cannot be stretched after it ends.

A plan that ends a day early, or one that empties on the busiest day, often costs more in the end than one sized correctly from the start, because you buy a second plan to cover the gap.

  • Check whether your phone supports eSIM before comparing prices.
  • Use daily plans for uncertain usage patterns.
  • Use fixed-data plans when your usage is predictable.
  • Compare total data, validity, and route complexity together.

How to estimate usage without overbuying

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For comparing data plans before checkout, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

Réunion eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 0.90 USD per GB and 0.60 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 16.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 65.50 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
