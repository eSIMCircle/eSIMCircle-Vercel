Comparing Faroe Islands eSIMs on coverage, validity and setup

Three things decide whether a Faroe Islands plan fades into the background of the trip: coverage, validity and setup.

Published December 5, 2025 · Updated October 18, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

What to prepare before the first airport transfer

This section keeps the focus on QR setup, activation, and phone settings for Faroe Islands. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

Where this Faroe Islands trip will actually use data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For QR setup, activation, and phone settings, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

  • Turn data roaming on for the travel eSIM line only after you are ready to connect.
  • Save the QR code and manual activation details offline before departure.
  • Keep your primary SIM active for banking SMS if you need two-factor login during arrival.
  • Do not delete the eSIM profile after installation unless support asks you to.

Small setup choices that prevent big delays

Set up the eSIM while you still have steady Wi-Fi and time to read the prompts. Save the QR email, name the travel line clearly, select it for mobile data, and turn on roaming for that line if the instructions require it. If service does not appear immediately, restart the phone and wait for network registration before deleting anything.

A failed installation is cheap to fix at home and expensive in an arrivals hall. Do it while Wi-Fi is stable, keep the QR email until the trip is over, and leave the profile on the phone unless support tells you to remove it.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

The list for Faroe Islands has 10 plans: 4 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.69 to 15.69 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 5.55 to 55.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Faroe Islands eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.78 USD per GB and 1.85 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 26.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 156.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
