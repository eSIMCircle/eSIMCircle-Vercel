A Jordan eSIM sized to the length of the trip

Start with the calendar. The number of days in Jordan, including the two travel days, sets the plan.

Published August 1, 2025 · Updated September 16, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Line selection, roaming, and hotspot settings

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For QR setup, activation, and phone settings, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

What can go wrong if the plan is chosen too quickly

The risky shortcut is buying the cheapest plan before checking the boring details. Confirm that the phone is eSIM compatible and carrier-unlocked, then check whether the plan covers the full route, the full date range, and the kind of use implied by QR setup, activation, and phone settings. A little extra checking before checkout is easier than troubleshooting with no data after arrival.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

  • Keep the QR email until the trip is over.
  • Set the eSIM as the mobile data line, not necessarily the voice line.
  • Check that the device is carrier-unlocked and eSIM compatible.
  • Install on Wi-Fi before the trip when possible.

The Jordan plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Jordan has 12 plans: 4 priced per day and 8 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.49 to 15.59 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.55 to 56.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Jordan eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.83 USD per GB and 1.89 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 24.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 155.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

Is the least expensive Jordan plan enough?

Often, for a short trip with light use. For longer trips, a plan with a little room left over tends to be the calmer choice.

What is the least a Jordan plan costs here?

2.49 USD, for Jordan eSIM 500MB/Day.

Can more data be added along the way?

Yes. Any plan can be topped up or extended along the way, as long as it is still active.

How many plans are there to choose from?

12. 4 reset daily and 8 hold a fixed amount.

Is identity verification required?

Not for standard purchases.

Can the home SIM stay active?

Yes. Many travelers keep it for calls and SMS and use the eSIM for data.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.
