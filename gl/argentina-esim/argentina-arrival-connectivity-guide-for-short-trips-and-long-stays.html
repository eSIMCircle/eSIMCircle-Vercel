Landing in Argentina with data, for a short or long stay

Whether you stay a week or a season in Argentina, it helps to land with the phone already online.

Published May 4, 2025 · Updated September 29, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For price checks and roaming savings, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

Build the data plan around the real route

Build the plan around the first day that can go wrong. In Argentina, that may be landing late, finding transport, checking into a hotel, or moving between stops before you have trusted Wi-Fi. A plan that covers those awkward hours is usually better than one that only looks good on a price table. For price checks and roaming savings, count the travel days from arrival time to the last transfer, not just the nights in the destination.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

The list for Argentina has 12 plans: 4 priced per day and 8 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.69 to 16.29 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.99 to 47.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Argentina eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.40 USD per GB and 1.60 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 26.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 162.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

What should I turn on after landing?

The eSIM as your data line, with roaming on for it.

What is the least an Argentina plan costs here?

2.69 USD, for Argentina eSIM 500MB/Day.

If it does not connect?

Wait a little, restart, and check the eSIM is chosen for data.

How many plans are there to choose from?

12. 4 reset daily and 8 hold a fixed amount.

Is identity verification required?

Not for standard purchases.

Can the home SIM stay active?

Yes. Many travelers keep it for calls and SMS and use the eSIM for data.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.
