Paraguay data for hotspots, maps and an evening film

Maps by day, a shared hotspot in the afternoon, a film in the evening. That kind of Paraguay trip needs a roomier plan.

Published January 20, 2026 · Updated September 20, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Sharing data with laptops or family devices

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For maps, messages, ride apps, and hotspot use, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

The long-trip habits that reduce waste

This section keeps the focus on maps, messages, ride apps, and hotspot use for Paraguay. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

A longer stay adds uncertainty, and the answer to uncertainty is flexibility. Pick a plan you can extend, size it to the routine you expect rather than the first week, and add days when the trip grows.

  • Pick validity that covers the full stay rather than buying too short.
  • Download offline maps for long rural routes.
  • Use cloud backup on Wi-Fi when possible.
  • Check hotspot needs before choosing a plan.

The Paraguay plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Paraguay has 10 plans: 3 priced per day and 7 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 4.39 to 11.29 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 2GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.99 to 53.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Paraguay eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.68 USD per GB and 1.78 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 43.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (2GB per day) to 112.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

Who are daily plans for?

Travelers whose phones work hard every day: a laptop on hotspot, video calls, regular uploads.

What is the least a Paraguay plan costs here?

2.99 USD, for Paraguay eSIM 100MB 7 Days.

May I share the connection?

Each plan has its own hotspot rules, listed on the plan page.

How many plans are there to choose from?

10. 3 reset daily and 7 hold a fixed amount.

Can the home SIM stay in the phone?

Yes. Keep it for calls and messages and use the eSIM for data.

Can I buy for the whole group at once?

Yes. Several eSIMs can be bought in one purchase.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.
