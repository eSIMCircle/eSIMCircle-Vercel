Traveling Guam without roaming charges

Two settings and a prepaid eSIM are usually enough to keep roaming charges away on a Guam trip.

Published September 8, 2025 · Updated August 1, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For tourist data use before and after arrival, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Where savings, flexibility, and convenience meet

This section keeps the focus on tourist data use before and after arrival for Guam. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

The Guam plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Guam has 10 plans: 4 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 4.39 to 15.79 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 3GB per day. Fixed plans run from 6.55 to 74.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Guam eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 3.75 USD per GB and 2.50 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 48.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (3GB per day) to 157.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
