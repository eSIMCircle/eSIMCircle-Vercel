What to download before weak-signal moments

This section keeps the focus on tourist data use before and after arrival for Haiti. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

Plan selection notes for this article scenario

Read the plan cards as a fit check, not a scoreboard. Fixed-data plans are useful when your schedule and data habits are predictable. Daily or flexible plans are better when some days may be heavier than others. Compare Haiti eSIM options by validity, data ceiling, hotspot expectations, and setup timing before price becomes the deciding factor.

Plans are sold in steps of days, and trips rarely match them exactly. When a trip falls between two steps, the longer plan is usually the safer choice, because a plan can be extended while it is active but cannot be stretched after it ends.

A plan that ends a day early, or one that empties on the busiest day, often costs more in the end than one sized correctly from the start, because you buy a second plan to cover the gap.

  • Restart the phone after long border or ferry transfers.
  • Avoid assuming indoor basement coverage will match street-level signal.
  • Keep messaging apps ready for lower-signal moments.
  • Download maps before remote routes.
