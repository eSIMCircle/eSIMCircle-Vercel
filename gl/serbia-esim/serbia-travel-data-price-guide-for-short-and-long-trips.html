The price of travel data in Serbia, for short and long trips

Price makes sense only next to the days and data a plan gives. Here is how they line up for Serbia.

Published May 28, 2026 · Updated September 18, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Usage patterns that change the best choice

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For tourist data use before and after arrival, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Why the cheapest option is not always the safest

This section keeps the focus on tourist data use before and after arrival for Serbia. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

Price is one number on a list that also has days and data. Read all three before you choose. A plan that costs a little more but covers every day and your busiest day is better value than a cheaper one that leaves a gap.

Validity is counted in days, so count arrival and departure days as well as the nights in between. A flight that lands late or leaves early can touch more calendar dates than the stay itself, and a plan that ends the morning of departure has ended a day early.

  • Use Wi-Fi for large app updates and video backups.
  • Choose extra data if you expect hotspot use.
  • Check whether top-up is available for the selected plan.
  • Match validity to the full trip, including arrival and departure days.

The Serbia plans

The list for Serbia has 11 plans: 4 priced per day and 7 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 1.89 to 7.79 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.55 to 22.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Serbia eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 1.13 USD per GB and 0.75 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 18.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 77.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
