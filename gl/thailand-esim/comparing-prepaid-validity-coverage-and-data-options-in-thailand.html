Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For comparing data plans before checkout, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

Build the data plan around the real route

Build the plan around the first day that can go wrong. In Thailand, that may be landing late, finding transport, checking into a hotel, or moving between stops before you have trusted Wi-Fi. A plan that covers those awkward hours is usually better than one that only looks good on a price table. For comparing data plans before checkout, count the travel days from arrival time to the last transfer, not just the nights in the destination.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

The Thailand plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Thailand has 28 plans: 11 priced per day and 17 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 1.89 to 13.39 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.55 to 57.99 USD, with validity of 7, 10, 15, 30 or 180 days.

Among the fixed plans, Thailand eSIM 50GB 10 Days is the cheapest per gigabyte, at about 0.32 USD per GB. The longest, Thailand eSIM 50GB 180 Days, works out to about 0.32 USD per day over 180 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 10-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 18.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 133.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

Where should a comparison begin?

With the number of days the plan lasts. Then the data, then the price.

What is the least a Thailand plan costs here?

1.89 USD, for Thailand eSIM 500MB/Day.

Does my phone need to be unlocked?

Yes, and it needs to support eSIM.

How many plans are there to choose from?

28. 11 reset daily and 17 hold a fixed amount.

Is identity verification required?

Not for standard purchases.

Can the home SIM stay active?

Yes. Many travelers keep it for calls and SMS and use the eSIM for data.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.
