Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For common coverage and activation questions answered, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Phone settings that matter on travel eSIMs

Set up the eSIM while you still have steady Wi-Fi and time to read the prompts. Save the QR email, name the travel line clearly, select it for mobile data, and turn on roaming for that line if the instructions require it. If service does not appear immediately, restart the phone and wait for network registration before deleting anything.

When a phone shows no mobile data after landing, check the settings before blaming the network. Confirm the eSIM is selected as the line for mobile data and that data roaming is on for that line. Then give it a few minutes, restart the phone, and check the selection again.

Plan the installation for a day or two before you fly. That is early enough to sort out a problem, and late enough that the QR email is still easy to find.

The USA and Canada plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for USA and Canada has 9 plans: 3 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.59 to 4.99 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 2GB per day. Fixed plans run from 5.55 to 54.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

USA & Canada eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.73 USD per GB and 1.82 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 25.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (2GB per day) to 49.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
