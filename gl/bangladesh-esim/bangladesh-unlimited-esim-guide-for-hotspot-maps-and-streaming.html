Bangladesh data for hotspots, maps and an evening film

Maps by day, a shared hotspot in the afternoon, a film in the evening. That kind of Bangladesh trip needs a roomier plan.

Published April 6, 2026 · Updated August 1, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

How to keep data predictable over time

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For maps, messages, ride apps, and hotspot use, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

How eSIMCircle plan options fit this scenario

Read the plan cards as a fit check, not a scoreboard. Fixed-data plans are useful when your schedule and data habits are predictable. Daily or flexible plans are better when some days may be heavier than others. Compare Bangladesh eSIM options by validity, data ceiling, hotspot expectations, and setup timing before price becomes the deciding factor.

Validity is counted in days, so count arrival and departure days as well as the nights in between. A flight that lands late or leaves early can touch more calendar dates than the stay itself, and a plan that ends the morning of departure has ended a day early.

Price is one number on a list that also has days and data. Read all three before you choose. A plan that costs a little more but covers every day and your busiest day is better value than a cheaper one that leaves a gap.

The list for Bangladesh has 10 plans: 5 priced per day and 5 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 1.89 to 7.79 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 3.55 to 24.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Bangladesh eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 1.25 USD per GB and 0.83 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 18.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 77.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
