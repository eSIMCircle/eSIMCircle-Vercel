A few unhurried checks before buying make the rest of the Canada trip simpler.

Published July 21, 2026 · Updated October 2, 2026 · 3 minutes

Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For QR setup, activation, and phone settings, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Phone settings that matter on travel eSIMs

Set up the eSIM while you still have steady Wi-Fi and time to read the prompts. Save the QR email, name the travel line clearly, select it for mobile data, and turn on roaming for that line if the instructions require it. If service does not appear immediately, restart the phone and wait for network registration before deleting anything.

Keep your home SIM in the phone for calls and bank messages, and let the eSIM carry the data. Two lines in one phone is normal, and the settings that matter are which line handles mobile data and whether roaming is on for the eSIM line.

Plan the installation for a day or two before you fly. That is early enough to sort out a problem, and late enough that the QR email is still easy to find.

The Canada plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Canada has 23 plans: 9 priced per day and 14 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.39 to 13.29 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.55 to 54.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Canada eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 1.58 USD per GB and 1.05 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 23.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 132.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

Where should a comparison begin?

With the number of days the plan lasts. Then the data, then the price.

What is the least a Canada plan costs here?

2.39 USD, for Canada eSIM 500MB/Day.

Does my phone need to be unlocked?

Yes, and it needs to support eSIM.

How many plans are there to choose from?

23. 9 reset daily and 14 hold a fixed amount.

Is identity verification required?

Not for standard purchases.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.

Does the phone need anything special?

It needs eSIM support and must be unlocked by your home carrier.
