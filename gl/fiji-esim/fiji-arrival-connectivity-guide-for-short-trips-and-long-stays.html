Landing in Fiji with data, for a short or long stay

Whether you stay a week or a season in Fiji, it helps to land with the phone already online.

Published September 16, 2025 · Updated August 13, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For tourist data use before and after arrival, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Build the data plan around the real route

Build the plan around the first day that can go wrong. In Fiji, that may be landing late, finding transport, checking into a hotel, or moving between stops before you have trusted Wi-Fi. A plan that covers those awkward hours is usually better than one that only looks good on a price table. For tourist data use before and after arrival, count the travel days from arrival time to the last transfer, not just the nights in the destination.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

The Fiji plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Fiji has 10 plans: 4 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.99 to 14.29 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 4.99 to 52.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Fiji eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.63 USD per GB and 1.75 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 29.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 142.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
