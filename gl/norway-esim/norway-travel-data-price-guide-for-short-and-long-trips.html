The price of travel data in Norway, for short and long trips

Price makes sense only next to the days and data a plan gives. Here is how they line up for Norway.

Published November 29, 2025 · Updated September 16, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Usage patterns that change the best choice

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For 7-day, 15-day, and 30-day plan timing, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Why the cheapest option is not always the safest

This section keeps the focus on 7-day, 15-day, and 30-day plan timing for Norway. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

A plan that ends a day early, or one that empties on the busiest day, often costs more in the end than one sized correctly from the start, because you buy a second plan to cover the gap.

The simplest check is to write the trip as a list of dates and put the plan's validity beside it. If the plan falls short at either end, choose the next step up before you pay, not after the plan has expired.

  • Match validity to the full trip, including arrival and departure days.
  • Use Wi-Fi for large app updates and video backups.
  • Choose extra data if you expect hotspot use.
  • Check whether top-up is available for the selected plan.

The Norway plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Norway has 10 plans: 3 priced per day and 7 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.09 to 7.39 USD, with allowances from 1GB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.99 to 50.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15, 30 or 180 days.

Among the fixed plans, Norway eSIM 20GB 30 Days is the cheapest per gigabyte, at about 0.83 USD per GB. The longest, Norway eSIM 50GB 180 Days, works out to about 0.28 USD per day over 180 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (1GB per day) comes to 20.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 73.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.
