Landing in Ireland with data, for a short or long stay

Whether you stay a week or a season in Ireland, it helps to land with the phone already online.

Published September 28, 2025 · Updated October 2, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

What to prepare before the first airport transfer

This section keeps the focus on comparing data plans before checkout for Ireland. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

Where this Ireland trip will actually use data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For comparing data plans before checkout, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

  • Keep your primary SIM active for banking SMS if you need two-factor login during arrival.
  • Save the QR code and manual activation details offline before departure.
  • Turn data roaming on for the travel eSIM line only after you are ready to connect.
  • Do not delete the eSIM profile after installation unless support asks you to.

Small setup choices that prevent big delays

Set up the eSIM while you still have steady Wi-Fi and time to read the prompts. Save the QR email, name the travel line clearly, select it for mobile data, and turn on roaming for that line if the instructions require it. If service does not appear immediately, restart the phone and wait for network registration before deleting anything.

Two things make installation go smoothly: a stable Wi-Fi connection and the QR email saved somewhere you can open without mobile data. Everything else, including switching the eSIM on, can wait until you land.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

The Ireland plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Ireland has 22 plans: 4 priced per day and 18 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 1.89 to 6.89 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 3.55 to 84.99 USD, with validity of 3, 7, 15, 30 or 180 days.

Ireland eSIM 50GB 180 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 0.81 USD per GB and 0.23 USD per day over 180 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 4 days is one day longer than the 3-day plans, so the next fit is the 7-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 18.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 68.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

What should I turn on after landing?

The eSIM as your data line, with roaming on for it.

What is the least an Ireland plan costs here?

If it does not connect?

Wait a little, restart, and check the eSIM is chosen for data.

How many plans are there to choose from?

22. 4 reset daily and 18 hold a fixed amount.

Can the home SIM stay in the phone?

Yes. Keep it for calls and messages and use the eSIM for data.

Can a plan be extended mid-trip?

Yes. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is still active.
