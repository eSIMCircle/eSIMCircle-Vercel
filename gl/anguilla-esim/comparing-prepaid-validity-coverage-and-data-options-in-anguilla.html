Prepaid plans for Anguilla come down to days, place and data. Here they are, one at a time.

What to download before weak-signal moments

This section keeps the focus on maps, messages, ride apps, and hotspot use for Anguilla. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Plan selection notes for this article scenario

Read the plan cards as a fit check, not a scoreboard. Fixed-data plans are useful when your schedule and data habits are predictable. Daily or flexible plans are better when some days may be heavier than others. Compare Anguilla eSIM options by validity, data ceiling, hotspot expectations, and setup timing before price becomes the deciding factor.

Validity is counted in days, so count arrival and departure days as well as the nights in between. A flight that lands late or leaves early can touch more calendar dates than the stay itself, and a plan that ends the morning of departure has ended a day early.

Price is one number on a list that also has days and data. Read all three before you choose. A plan that costs a little more but covers every day and your busiest day is better value than a cheaper one that leaves a gap.

  • Avoid assuming indoor basement coverage will match street-level signal.
  • Keep messaging apps ready for lower-signal moments.
  • Download maps before remote routes.
  • Restart the phone after long border or ferry transfers.

The list for Anguilla has 9 plans: 3 priced per day and 6 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 4.99 to 12.69 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 2GB per day. Fixed plans run from 10.99 to 135.99 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Anguilla eSIM 20GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 6.80 USD per GB and 4.53 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 49.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (2GB per day) to 126.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

Where should a comparison begin?

With the number of days the plan lasts. Then the data, then the price.

What is the least an Anguilla plan costs here?

4.99 USD, for Anguilla eSIM 500MB/Day.

Does my phone need to be unlocked?

Yes, and it needs to support eSIM.

How many plans are there to choose from?

9. 3 reset daily and 6 hold a fixed amount.

Can the home SIM stay in the phone?

Yes. Keep it for calls and messages and use the eSIM for data.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.

Does the phone need anything special?

It needs eSIM support and must be unlocked by your home carrier.
