The price of travel data in Denmark, for short and long trips

Price makes sense only next to the days and data a plan gives. Here is how they line up for Denmark.

Published April 6, 2026 · Updated September 11, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

Where savings, flexibility, and convenience meet

This section keeps the focus on comparing data plans before checkout for Denmark. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

Which travel moments consume the most data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For comparing data plans before checkout, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

  • Use Wi-Fi for large app updates and video backups.
  • Match validity to the full trip, including arrival and departure days.
  • Check whether top-up is available for the selected plan.
  • Choose extra data if you expect hotspot use.

How eSIMCircle plan options fit this scenario

Read the plan cards as a fit check, not a scoreboard. Fixed-data plans are useful when your schedule and data habits are predictable. Daily or flexible plans are better when some days may be heavier than others. Compare Denmark eSIM options by validity, data ceiling, hotspot expectations, and setup timing before price becomes the deciding factor.

Plans are sold in steps of days, and trips rarely match them exactly. When a trip falls between two steps, the longer plan is usually the safer choice, because a plan can be extended while it is active but cannot be stretched after it ends.

Price is one number on a list that also has days and data. Read all three before you choose. A plan that costs a little more but covers every day and your busiest day is better value than a cheaper one that leaves a gap.

The Denmark plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Denmark has 11 plans: 4 priced per day and 7 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.29 to 6.89 USD, with allowances from 1GB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.99 to 40.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15, 30 or 180 days.

Denmark eSIM 50GB 180 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 0.81 USD per GB and 0.23 USD per day over 180 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (1GB per day) comes to 22.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 68.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

Is the least expensive Denmark plan enough?
