A first trip to Mexico, with the phone ready

A first trip to Mexico is easier with one less thing to figure out on arrival.

Published January 20, 2026 · Updated October 14, 2026 · 3 minutes

* * *

How the itinerary changes mobile data needs

Build the plan around the first day that can go wrong. In Mexico, that may be landing late, finding transport, checking into a hotel, or moving between stops before you have trusted Wi-Fi. A plan that covers those awkward hours is usually better than one that only looks good on a price table. For tourist data use before and after arrival, count the travel days from arrival time to the last transfer, not just the nights in the destination.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

How to estimate usage without overbuying

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For tourist data use before and after arrival, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

The Mexico plans

As listed when these notes were last updated. The plan page keeps the current prices and terms, and any plan can be topped up or extended while it runs.

The list for Mexico has 13 plans: 4 priced per day and 9 with a fixed allowance. Daily plans run from 2.49 to 14.19 USD, with allowances from 500MB to 10GB per day. Fixed plans run from 2.55 to 101.55 USD, with validity of 7, 15 or 30 days.

Mexico eSIM 50GB 30 Days is both the longest fixed plan and the cheapest per gigabyte. It works out to about 2.03 USD per GB and 3.38 USD per day over 30 days.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 24.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (10GB per day) to 141.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

A few questions

When should I install it?

At home, on good Wi-Fi, a day or so before leaving.

What is the least a Mexico plan costs here?

2.49 USD, for Mexico eSIM 500MB/Day.

How does the eSIM arrive?

By email, with a QR code and instructions. Keep the email until you are home.

How many plans are there to choose from?

13. 4 reset daily and 9 hold a fixed amount.

Can the home SIM stay in the phone?

Yes. Keep it for calls and messages and use the eSIM for data.

Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?

Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.

Does the phone need anything special?

It needs eSIM support and must be unlocked by your home carrier.
