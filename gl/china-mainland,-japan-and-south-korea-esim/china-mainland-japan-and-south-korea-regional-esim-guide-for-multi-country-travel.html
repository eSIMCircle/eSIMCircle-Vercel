What to prepare before the first airport transfer

This section keeps the focus on best use cases for flights, layovers, and border crossings for China Mainland, Japan and South Korea. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

Where this China Mainland, Japan and South Korea trip will actually use data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For best use cases for flights, layovers, and border crossings, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

  • Save the QR code and manual activation details offline before departure.
  • Keep your primary SIM active for banking SMS if you need two-factor login during arrival.
  • Do not delete the eSIM profile after installation unless support asks you to.
  • Turn data roaming on for the travel eSIM line only after you are ready to connect.

Small setup choices that prevent big delays

Set up the eSIM while you still have steady Wi-Fi and time to read the prompts. Save the QR email, name the travel line clearly, select it for mobile data, and turn on roaming for that line if the instructions require it. If service does not appear immediately, restart the phone and wait for network registration before deleting anything.
