Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For price guide for regional travel data, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

If you cannot decide between two sizes, take the smaller one and keep the option to top up. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is active, so a first guess is allowed to be wrong.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Build the data plan around the real route

Build the plan around the first day that can go wrong. In China Mainland, Japan and South Korea, that may be landing late, finding transport, checking into a hotel, or moving between stops before you have trusted Wi-Fi. A plan that covers those awkward hours is usually better than one that only looks good on a price table. For price guide for regional travel data, count the travel days from arrival time to the last transfer, not just the nights in the destination.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.
