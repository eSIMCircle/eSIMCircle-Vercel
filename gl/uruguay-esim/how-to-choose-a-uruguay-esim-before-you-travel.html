Choosing between daily flexibility and fixed data

Mobile data disappears through ordinary actions: map refreshes, ride confirmations, translation, ticket screens, restaurant searches, and messages to the people waiting for you. For tourist data use before and after arrival, keep large updates, backups, and streaming on Wi-Fi. If a laptop hotspot or family sharing is part of the trip, choose more daily headroom than a maps-only traveler would need.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

Planning notes for Uruguay

Build the plan around the first day that can go wrong. In Uruguay, that may be landing late, finding transport, checking into a hotel, or moving between stops before you have trusted Wi-Fi. A plan that covers those awkward hours is usually better than one that only looks good on a price table. For tourist data use before and after arrival, count the travel days from arrival time to the last transfer, not just the nights in the destination.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

Why arrival and departure days count

This section keeps the focus on tourist data use before and after arrival for Uruguay. Before choosing, picture the exact moments when being offline would be annoying: arrival, transport, check-in, route changes, or a payment screen. The right eSIM is the one that covers those moments without making you overbuy for days when hotel Wi-Fi will do most of the work.

The simplest check is to write the trip as a list of dates and put the plan's validity beside it. If the plan falls short at either end, choose the next step up before you pay, not after the plan has expired.

Price is one number on a list that also has days and data. Read all three before you choose. A plan that costs a little more but covers every day and your busiest day is better value than a cheaper one that leaves a gap.
